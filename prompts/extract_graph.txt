-Goal-
Given a prior authorization policy text chunk and a list of allowed entity types, identify the policy entities and relationships that are useful for extracting brand-level access parameters.

The graph should support answers about:
- age requirements
- step therapy requirements
- number of branded or biologic steps
- number of generic or non-biologic steps
- phototherapy step-through
- TB testing
- initial authorization duration
- reauthorization duration
- reauthorization required
- reauthorization criteria
- specialist requirements
- quantity limits

-Important policy reasoning rules-
1. Preserve applicability. Capture whether a rule applies to a brand, indication, drug class, all indications, all brands, preferred products, non-preferred products, initial authorization, or reauthorization.
2. Preserve AND/OR logic. If a requirement has alternatives joined by OR, describe it as an alternative path. If requirements are cumulative or joined by AND, describe them as jointly required.
3. Preserve exceptions. Contraindication, intolerance, inadequate response, inability to use, continuation criteria, and other exceptions should become entities or be included in relationship descriptions.
4. Preserve severity. If the text distinguishes moderate-to-severe psoriasis from severe psoriasis, capture the severity level and link it to the relevant indication or rule.
5. Distinguish step types. Biologics, biosimilars, targeted drugs, named branded products, ustekinumab, adalimumab, and therapy classes count as branded/biologic step concepts. Topical agents and unnamed non-biologic requirements count as generic/non-biologic step concepts. Phototherapy is its own concept and should not be merged into branded or generic step counts.
6. Distinguish quantity limits from dosing. Extract a quantity limit only when the text explicitly frames it as a quantity limit or equivalent quantity-limit restriction.
7. Do not infer unsupported facts. Do not invent generic names, indications, drug classes, ages, durations, or relationships that are not supported by the text.

-Entity extraction-
For each identified entity, extract:
- entity_name: A concise canonical name, capitalized.
- entity_type: One of the following allowed types: [{entity_types}]
- entity_description: A comprehensive but evidence-grounded description of what the text says about this entity, including values, applicability, and important conditions.

Format each entity exactly as:
("entity"<|><entity_name><|><entity_type><|><entity_description>)

-Relationship extraction-
From the entities identified above, identify clearly supported relationships. Prefer relationships that help downstream extraction of PA parameters.

For each relationship, extract:
- source_entity: name of the source entity, as identified above
- target_entity: name of the target entity, as identified above
- relationship_description: include the relationship type in plain English, such as applies to, requires, has criterion, has exception, has duration, has quantity limit, requires specialist, requires lab test, is alternative to, is jointly required with, or is restricted to
- relationship_strength: 1-10, where 10 means the relationship is explicit and central to coverage, 5 means useful but contextual, and 1 means weakly related

Format each relationship exactly as:
("relationship"<|><source_entity><|><target_entity><|><relationship_description><|><relationship_strength>)

Return output in English as a single list of all entities and relationships. Use ## as the list delimiter.
When finished, output <|COMPLETE|>.
